Flop and Fawn
The Two Stress Responses
Nobody Talks About

You have heard of fight, flight and freeze

But there are two more that almost nobody talks about.
And they might be the most revealing of all.

02 — 06

The first is Flop

Complete shutdown

When the threat feels so overwhelming the nervous system does not fight, flee or freeze.

It collapses. Loss of control. Blacking out. Disorientation. The muscles literally give way. For the nervous system that experienced something truly unsurvivable, it was the only option left.

03 — 06

The second is Fawn
And this one is everywhere

Fawn is when you appease. When you make yourself agreeable — not because you want to, but because conflict feels dangerous.

04 — 06

What fawn looks like

Chronic people pleasing. Never being able to say no.

Putting everyone else's needs so far ahead of your own that you lose track of what you even want.

No boundaries. Shrinking yourself to keep the peace.

Caring so desperately about what others think that you stop being yourself entirely.

05 — 06

Fawn in adults who grew up
in unpredictable environments

When you could not control what happened, you learned to control how people felt about you.

Keeping everyone happy kept you safe. So the nervous system made it automatic.

And now even when perfectly safe — you are still performing. Still fawning.

06 — 06

Recognizing your response
is the first act
of freedom.
