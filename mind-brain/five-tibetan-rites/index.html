the five tibetan rites

— five movements, one breath —

spin · lift · arch · table · wave

morning · 10 to 20 minutes · a mat and a clear floor

how the practice runs

Five movements, done in order, each repeated the same number of times. The breath sets the pace: every rite rises on an in-breath and returns on an out-breath, so you never hold it and never rush it.

  • 3Start with three repetitions of each rite.
  • +2Add two repetitions a week, as long as the last ones still feel clean.
  • 21Stop at twenty-one. That is the full practice.
  • 2×Between rites, stand with your hands on your hips and take two slow, full breaths.

In each drawing the pencil figure is where the breath goes out, and the ink figure is where it comes in. Press breathe with it to watch one move.

rite one · standing · seen from the front 3 → 21 turns

the spin

Stand tall with your arms straight out to the sides, palms down, level with your shoulders. Turn to your right, clockwise, at a steady walking pace. Count each full turn.

breathe

Breathe evenly through the nose while you turn, about one breath for every two turns. Don't hold it to steady yourself.

afterStop, hands on hips, and take two full breaths before you lie down.

extend

Reach out through the fingertips as if someone were pulling each hand away from you. The crown of the head lifts. Shoulders stay down, away from the ears.

easier

Turn slowly and do fewer turns. Stop at the first sign of dizziness, even if that is two.

deeper

Spot like a dancer: hold your eyes on one point and whip the head round last. That is what lets you reach 21 without spinning out.

rite two · lying on the back · seen from the side 3 → 21 lifts

the leg lift

Lie flat on your back, arms along your sides, palms pressing the floor. Lift your head and tuck your chin to your chest while you raise both straight legs to vertical. Lower head and legs together, slowly, all the way down.

breathe

inthrough the nose as the head and legs rise, filling the belly first.

outfully as they lower. Let the whole body go soft on the floor before the next lift.

extend

Push the heels toward the ceiling with the knees straight and the toes softly flexed. The back of the neck lengthens as the chin tucks. The low back stays pressed into the floor.

easier

Bend the knees as you lift, or raise one leg at a time with the other foot flat. Keep the head down if the neck strains.

deeper

Let the legs travel a little past vertical, toward the face, without the hips leaving the floor. Take twice as long to lower as to lift.

rite three · kneeling · seen from the side 3 → 21 arches

the kneeling arch

Kneel with the knees hip-width apart, toes tucked under, torso upright. Rest your hands on the backs of your thighs, just under the buttocks. Tuck your chin, then arch backward, letting the head drop back. Come up to upright and tuck the chin again.

breathe

inas you arch back and the chest opens to the ceiling.

outas you come up and the chin returns to the chest.

extend

The whole front of the body lengthens: the fronts of the hips, the belly, the chest and the throat. Press the hips forward first so the arch comes from the upper back, not by folding at the waist.

easier

Fold a blanket under the knees. Make a small arch and keep the head in line with the spine instead of dropping it.

deeper

Slide the hands down the backs of the thighs as you arch. Hold the top of the arch for one extra breath before rising.

rite four · seated · seen from the side 3 → 21 lifts

the tabletop

Sit with your legs straight out, feet about hip-width apart, palms flat beside your hips with the fingers pointing toward your feet. Tuck your chin. Then press through hands and feet and lift the hips until torso and thighs make a flat table, knees bent over the ankles and head gently back. Lower back to sitting.

breathe

inas the hips rise into the table.

outas you sit back down and the chin tucks.

extend

Open the fronts of the shoulders and the hips. Squeeze the buttocks to bring the hips level with the knees, and let the throat lengthen as the head tips back.

easier

Start with the knees already bent and the feet flat. Lift the hips only partway, and keep the chin level instead of tipping the head.

deeper

Hold the table for two or three breaths at the top, tightening every muscle in the body, then release on the out-breath.

rite five · hands and feet · seen from the side 3 → 21 waves

the wave

Start face down, supported on your hands and the balls of your feet, hands under the shoulders, hips low and head up. Push the hips up and back into an inverted V, chin toward the chest. Then lower the hips again, swinging the chest forward and the head up. Only the hands and feet touch the floor.

breathe

indeeply as the hips rise into the V.

outfully as the hips sink and the chest comes forward.

extend

In the V, lengthen from the hands to the tailbone and press the heels toward the floor. In the low position, draw the chest forward between the arms and lengthen the whole spine into a long, even curve.

easier

Bend the knees in the V. In the low position, let the knees rest on the floor.

deeper

Heels reach the floor in the V. At the bottom, the hips hover just above the floor with the legs straight and active.

The Five Tibetan Rites come from Peter Kelder's 1939 booklet The Eye of Revelation. Their Tibetan origin has never been verified.

Rites two to five load the neck and lower back, and the spin can bring on dizziness. With back or neck problems, vertigo, heart conditions or high blood pressure, during pregnancy or after recent surgery, ask a doctor first or keep to the easier versions.
