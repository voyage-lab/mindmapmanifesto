    How this is calculated

    Fate x will graph

    1. Fate code. Write the day (two digits) and month (two digits) side by side as one number, then multiply by the year. Example: 21 · 01 · 1998 → 2101 × 1998 = 4197798.
    2. Will code. Replace every 0 in the written date with 1, then do the same multiplication. Example: 2111 × 1998 = 4217778.
    3. If a product has fewer than seven digits, a 0 is padded on the front; the first seven digits are used.
    4. Each digit (0–9) is one point on the line. The seven points sit 12 years apart, starting at your birth year: ages 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 and 72. Example: born 1980 → 1980, 1992, 2004, 2016, 2028, 2040, 2052.
    5. Where the fate line sits higher, the tradition reads the period as ruled by circumstance; where will sits higher, by your own choices; level stretches are read as turning points.

    Pythagorean square (psychomatrix)

    1. Take the eight digits of the date D D M M Y Y Y Y.
    2. W1 = their sum. W2 = the digits of W1 added together.
    3. W3 = W1 − 2 × (first digit of the day). W4 = the digits of W3 added together.
    4. Pool together the date digits and the digits of W1–W4, then count how many 1s, 2s … 9s there are. Zeros are ignored.
    5. Each count fills its cell; rows, columns and diagonals are summed for the secondary lines.

    Where the method comes from

    This is 20th-century popular numerology, most of it codified by Alexander Alexandrov in the 1990s. Pythagoras (c. 570–495 BCE) left no writings, and nothing resembling a birth-date square or a fate/will digit graph appears in any ancient source about him — the name is honorific. The 7-year cycle you may see elsewhere belongs to a different chart (the "graph of life", built from day × month × year with one digit per year); the fate x will graph always uses 12-year steps.

    For entertainment. There is no scientific basis for any of this, and nothing here is advice.
