## Rule 01 — The 3 Foundational Rules of the Mind

Your mind is either working for you or against you.

- **Working against you:** When you don't know the rules — it runs on autopilot.
- **Working for you:** When you understand it — you become the one giving instructions.

### Rule 1 — Your mind does exactly what it *thinks* you want — not what you actually want.

Keep saying these, and your mind goes — *got it, delivering more:*

- "I am so stressed."
- "I am so broke."
- "I am so tired."

### Rule 2 — Your mind only responds to two things.

1. The pictures you make in your head
2. The words you say to yourself

Change those two things and you literally start rewiring your reality.

### Rule 3 — Your mind *loves* what is familiar and *hates* what is unfamiliar.

This is why change feels so hard. But it is not weakness, not laziness — just your mind doing its job, protecting what it already knows.

**The good news?** Not motivation. Not willpower. Repetition makes new things familiar.

> Three rules. One mind. Infinite possibility — once you understand how it works.

## Rule 02 — Your Mind Only Responds to 2 Things

Most people have no idea what they are.

1. **The pictures** — The images you make and play inside your head
2. **The words** — The things you say to yourself, all day, every day

That is it. Nothing else. Not what happens to you. Not your circumstances. Not other people.

Which means you have way more control than you think.

### Your mind cannot tell the difference between *real* and *vividly imagined*.

- **Words at work:** A song takes you back. You hear it and you are instantly there — the memory, the feeling, the moment.
- **Pictures at work:** The thing has not happened — but your body is already reacting to the picture you are playing in your head.

Real or imagined. Your mind treats both the same way.

### Want to feel more confident?

1. **Picture** yourself being confident — vividly, in detail
2. **Talk** to yourself like someone who already is

Your mind will follow. It has no choice. That is literally all it responds to.

> Two things. That is your whole toolkit. Use them deliberately.

## Rule 03 — Your Mind Does What It Thinks You Want

It is not broken. It is taking your orders.

Every time you say it, your mind hears it as an instruction:

| What you say | What your mind hears |
|---|---|
| "I always mess things up." | Understood → delivering more of this |
| "I can never stick to anything." | Understood → confirming this pattern |
| "I am just not a confident person." | Understood → reinforcing this identity |

It is not punishing you. It genuinely thinks that is what you want.

### Your mind is a search engine.

It finds evidence for whatever you type in:

- Type in *"I am a failure"* → It finds every single failure. Proof everywhere.
- Type in *"I figure things out"* → It finds evidence of that instead. Every time.

It does not care if what you tell it is true or false. It just runs the search.

### Notice the dramatic words. Then find one that costs less.

| Catastrophic | → | Manageable |
|---|---|---|
| "I am devastated" | → | "I am disappointed" |
| "I am a disaster" | → | "I am still figuring it out" |
| "I always fail" | → | "This one did not work" |

Small word. Huge difference to your nervous system.

> Your mind is always listening. Start giving it better instructions.

## Rule 04 — Imagination Is More Powerful Than Logic

The most misunderstood rule of the mind.

You cannot **think** your way out of something you have **felt** your way into. In the battle between emotion and logic — emotion wins. Every single time.

### Logic talks to the *wrong* part of your brain.

The part actually running the show runs on feeling and imagination.

- You know smoking is bad for you — but the craving doesn't care
- You know the presentation will be fine — but the panic still comes
- You know your ex wasn't right for you — but you still check their Instagram

### Stop trying to logic yourself into change.

Instead — use the right language. **Imagine it. Feel it.**

Picture the version of you who already has what you want. Ask yourself:

1. How do they **stand?**
2. How do they **talk?**
3. How do they **see themselves?**

That is the language your mind actually speaks.

> Stop arguing with yourself. Start imagining a better story.

## Rule 05 — The 5 Things Every Child Needs

A framework for understanding the psyche.

### Every child needs five things:

1. To feel **significant**
2. To feel **loved**
3. To feel they **matter**
4. To feel **celebrated**
5. To feel their parents are **proud**

When a child cannot get these needs met, they draw a conclusion — *I cannot.* That conclusion follows them into adulthood:

- Because I felt **unsafe** — I cannot take risks.
- Because I felt **unlovable** — I hold myself back.
- Because I felt **unworthy** — I do not aim high.

But you are no longer that child. That changes everything.

## Rule 06 — The Belief Behind Almost Every Problem You Have

The root beneath all limiting beliefs.

There is one belief sitting underneath almost every problem you have ever had. And most people never even know it is there.

### I am not enough.

Not smart enough. Not lovable enough. Not interesting, attractive, worthy enough. Beneath the surface — it is almost always the same root.

### People who feel they are not enough spend their whole lives looking for *more.*

More money. More attention. More food. More validation. More of anything. No amount of external things can fix an internal belief. The void stays until the belief changes.

### This belief was not born in you.

- **The learned belief:** I must earn my worth. I need to be more, do more, prove more — only then will I be enough.
- **The original truth:** Watch any baby. They do not doubt their worth. Enough-ness was your starting point.

It was **learned.** Which means it can be **unlearned.**

**Your affirmation:**
> *I am enough. I have always been enough. I was born enough.*

Say it. Write it. Repeat it. When you transform this belief — you transform everything.

## Rule 07 — Why You Tell Yourself What You Want Is Not Available

The second great limiting belief.

*"It is not available to me."*

It sounds like realism. It feels like realism. But it is a belief — handed to you before you were old enough to question it.

- "I want love, but it never works out for me."
- "I want success, but I did not come from the right background."
- "I want to be healthy, but it runs in my family."

### We learn what we live. We live what we learn.

You absorbed that certain things were *not really yours to have.*

### This belief is sneaky.

1. Brushing off compliments as though they were meant for someone else
2. Rationalizing why good things could not possibly last

Someone says you did an amazing job and you say — *"Oh, I just got lucky. Anyone could have done it."* That is not modesty. That is a belief running in the background.

**Your practice today:** Praise yourself. For something. Anything. Because self-praise is always available to you — no matter what you were taught.

> Self-praise is always available to you — no matter what you were taught.

## Rule 08 — The Fear That Has Been Running You Since Childhood

The third great limiting belief.

Your brain processes social rejection the same as a threat to your life. When we feel *different,* we cannot connect — and connection is everything to the human mind.

Feeling excluded was treated as a life or death situation. Being different was absolutely devastating.

### This fear shows up everywhere:

- Staying **quiet** when you have something to say
- **Hiding** parts of yourself to be accepted
- **Shrinking** so others feel more comfortable

All of it tracing back to that one childhood conclusion.

### Here is the reframe.

Every person alive is afraid of being different. Your difference is not proof of isolation — it is proof that you are exactly the same as everyone else. Your difference is proof of belonging.

> Your difference is not your flaw. It is your proof of belonging.

## Rule 09 — Why Change Feels So Hard

Something inside you is actively fighting back.

Your mind loves what is **familiar.** It hates what is unfamiliar.

- Familiar feels like: Safety. Home. Known. Comfortable — even when it hurts.
- Unfamiliar feels like: Threat. Danger. Risk. Something to be avoided — even when it's growth.

That is how your brain was wired to survive.

### This is why you go back — not because you are:

- Weak
- Lacking desire for better
- Broken in some way

But because your mind is just doing its job — keeping you inside what it knows.

### The secret is not willpower.

- **What doesn't work:** Forcing yourself. White-knuckling it. Trying harder with the same approach.
- **What actually works:** Repetition. Make the unfamiliar familiar. Do it enough — it becomes the new comfort zone.

Your mind stops seeing it as a threat. It becomes **the new normal.**

**Your daily question:** *What am I making familiar today?*

> You do not need more willpower. You need more repetition.

## Rule 10 — How Your Childhood Needs Are Running Your Adult Life

The patterns you cannot seem to break almost always have a starting point.

When childhood needs go unmet, one of two paths forms:

- **Path one:** They give up on their needs entirely — concluding they will never be met.
- **Path two:** They give their needs away — spending a lifetime searching for someone else to fulfil them.

### You see it everywhere once you know what to look for:

- The **overachiever** — constantly proving their worth to a parent who never acknowledged them.
- The **people pleaser** — terrified of disapproval because disapproval once meant withdrawal of love.
- The person who **always needs to be needed** — because significance was the one thing they never felt.

### None of these are personality traits. They are strategies.

Survival strategies that outlived the situation they were built for.

### Here is what changes everything:

Recognizing that you are **no longer a dependent child.** You do not need the same people to give you what they could not back then. You can choose differently now.

You can provide for yourself now:

- **Love** — no longer out of your hands.
- **Significance** — no longer out of your hands.
- **Worthiness** — no longer out of your hands.

> The wound was real. But so is your ability to heal it.

## Rule 11 — You Were Born Enough

Not born with self-doubt. Not born with insecurity. Not born feeling like you had to earn your place. Not born performing for approval or shrinking to make others comfortable.

You were born **enough.**

### Watch a baby.

They do not wonder if they are worthy of love. They do not apologize for taking up space. They simply exist — with complete certainty that they matter. That was you once. That is still you underneath everything that was layered on top.

### At some point the world started giving you feedback.

A parent who was too busy. A teacher who humiliated you. A friend who left without explanation. And your young mind landed on the cruelest possible answer — *it must be me.*

But that was **not the truth.**

That was a conclusion made by a child with limited information and no other explanation available. You believed it so long it started to feel like fact. It is not.

### Your mind learns by repetition.

Write this down. Say it out loud. Repeat it until your mind accepts it.

> *I am enough. I have always been enough.*

## Rule 12 — The Two Forces Controlling Every Decision You Make

Every habit, every choice, every reaction — two forces:

- **Force one:** Pain — moving away from what hurts.
- **Force two:** Pleasure — moving toward what feels good.

Your mind has one primary job: Keep you alive. And it does that by constantly moving you away from pain and toward pleasure.

### Here is what most people miss.

The two forces are **not equal.** Avoiding pain is a significantly stronger drive than seeking pleasure — because avoiding pain is how we stayed alive. Your mind will always prioritise pain avoidance over moving toward something good.

### This is why people stay stuck.

- The **pain of change** feels more immediate than the pleasure of a better life.
- The **pain of rejection** feels more real than the pleasure of connection.
- The **pain of failure** feels more certain than the pleasure of success.

Your mind is not being negative. It is being protective. There is a difference.

Once you understand this you stop fighting yourself — and start working **with** your mind instead of against it.

> Tell it what you want. Show it the pain of staying is greater than the pain of change.

## Rule 13 — Why Your Brain Is Wired to Stay Stuck

You know what you want. You know what you need to do. So why do you not do it?

Your mind is not designed for **happiness.** It is designed for **survival.** The single most effective survival strategy it has ever found is avoiding pain. Not seeking pleasure. Not chasing goals. Avoiding pain. That is the dominant program running in everything you do.

### Think about the goals you have not started.

The risks you have not taken. The conversations you keep postponing. It is not laziness. Your brain ran a calculation — and decided the pain of trying and failing outweighed the pleasure of succeeding.

### The part that stings.

The pain your brain is protecting you from is often **not even real.** It is a memory. A moment where trying led to embarrassment or rejection. And your brain filed it away as — *do not do that again.*

> Give your mind a more compelling reason to move. Make the pain of staying feel more real than the pain of change.

## Rule 14 — The Fight Response

And How It Is Showing Up In Your Life Right Now.

You probably think of fighting as something physical — but most fighting looks nothing like that.

When aggression felt like the only way to feel safe, the nervous system remembers what worked. So it keeps using it long after the danger is gone.

### In adulthood the fight response does not always look like anger:

- Needing to **control everything.**
- Expecting perfection from the people around you.
- An **unpredictable temper** that surprises even you.
- Dominating conversations. Dismissing other perspectives.

### None of it feels like fear.

But underneath — that is exactly what it is. A nervous system still fighting a threat that no longer exists.

- The need to control is almost always a fear of being out of control.
- The anger is almost always pain in disguise.

You cannot **logic** your way out of a nervous system response. But you can learn to recognize it.

The moment you feel the urge to control, criticize or overpower — pause.

> *"What am I actually afraid of right now?"*

## Rule 15 — The Flight Response

And How It Is Secretly Running Your Life.

You do not have to physically run away to be in flight mode. Most people are fleeing without ever leaving the room.

When something feels too overwhelming to confront, the nervous system finds another option — *run.* Not always physically. But mentally. Emotionally. Get away from the threat.

### In adulthood, flight does not look like running:

- **Staying perpetually busy** so you never have to sit with yourself.
- **Worrying constantly** — catastrophizing the future so you don't have to feel the present.
- **Perfectionism** — because if everything is perfect, nothing can hurt you.
- Anxiety. Obsessive thinking. The inability to ever truly relax.

### What makes flight so exhausting.

You can never outrun yourself. The thing you are fleeing is inside you. No matter how busy you get, how much you achieve — it is still there. Waiting.

### The antidote to flight is not stillness for its own sake.

It is **safety.** Teaching your nervous system that it is safe to slow down. Safe to feel. Safe to stop running.

> All of it — a nervous system trying to outrun something it never fully escaped.

## Rule 16 — The Freeze Response

When Your Mind Just Shuts Down.

Ever had a moment where you just could not move? That was not weakness. That was **freeze.**

When fight feels too dangerous and flight feels impossible, the nervous system picks a third option. Freeze. Shut down. Go still. Disappear into yourself.

### In adulthood, freeze does not look like standing still:

- **Chronic indecision** — spiralling over even simple choices.
- **Brain fog** — heavy and cloudy, thinking feels like wading through concrete.
- **Isolation** — withdrawing from people, hiding away.
- A persistent lack of motivation — not because you don't care, but because doing nothing felt safer.

### The most misunderstood response.

From the outside it looks like laziness. Apathy. Disengagement. But on the inside it feels like being trapped. Wanting to move and genuinely not being able to. The mind is not being difficult. It is being protective.

### The way out is not to push harder.

It is to create enough **safety** that your nervous system finally believes it is okay to move again. Small steps. Small decisions. Small proof that action does not lead to danger.

> You are not broken. You are not lazy. You are a nervous system waiting to feel safe enough to move.

## Rule 17 — Flop and Fawn

The Two Stress Responses Nobody Talks About.

You have heard of fight, flight and freeze — but there are **two more** that almost nobody talks about. And they might be the most revealing of all.

### The first is Flop — Complete shutdown.

When the threat feels so overwhelming the nervous system does not fight, flee or freeze. It **collapses.** Loss of control. Blacking out. Disorientation. The muscles literally give way. For the nervous system that experienced something truly unsurvivable, it was the only option left.

### The second is Fawn — And this one is everywhere.

Fawn is when you appease. When you make yourself agreeable — not because you want to, but because conflict feels dangerous.

**What fawn looks like:**

- Chronic people pleasing. Never being able to say no.
- Putting everyone else's needs so far ahead of your own that you lose track of what you even want.
- No boundaries. Shrinking yourself to keep the peace.
- Caring so desperately about what others think that you stop being yourself entirely.

### Fawn in adults who grew up in unpredictable environments.

When you could not control what happened, you learned to control how people felt about you. Keeping everyone happy kept you safe. So the nervous system made it automatic. And now even when perfectly safe — you are still performing. Still fawning.

> Recognizing your response is the first act of freedom.

## Rule 18 — Why Your Brain Keeps Playing the Same Negative Thoughts

There is a reason it will not leave.

Every thought creates a physical reaction and an emotional response. That emotion drives your actions. Your actions provoke more thoughts.

### This is called a looping thought.

*"I cannot do anything right."* That makes me feel anxious. I feel anxious because I am stupid. I am stupid because I cannot do anything right. And back to the beginning.

The thought feeds the feeling. The feeling feeds the thought. The whole loop tightens.

### Your mind protects the loop instead of breaking it — because it loves what is familiar.

This is why you cannot simply think yourself out of it. In the battle between emotion and logic — **emotion wins.**

### The way out is not to fight the thought.

It is to **interrupt it.** Choose a better thought. Not a perfect one — just a slightly better one. Then repeat it. Until that becomes the loop instead.

> The longer a loop runs, the more hardwired it becomes. Until it does not feel like a thought anymore. It feels like you.

## Rule 19 — How to Break a Negative Thought Loop

You cannot stop a looping thought by telling yourself to stop thinking it. Here is what actually works.

### Your mind does not respond well to negatives.

Tell yourself *"do not think about that"* — and your mind immediately does the opposite. Because you just gave it a picture and a word — the two things it responds to most. Fighting the thought only makes the thought stronger.

### So you stop fighting. And you start replacing.

Think of it as a **ladder.** You do not jump from *"I am terrible at this"* straight to *"I am amazing at this."* Your mind will not buy it. The gap is too big.

### Climb one rung at a time:

| Old thought | → | Better thought |
|---|---|---|
| "I am terrible at this" | → | "I am still learning this." |
| "I always mess up" | → | "I do not always get it right." |
| "I am a failure" | → | "I am someone who keeps trying." |

This works because you are not asking your mind to believe something it cannot accept yet. Just something **slightly less painful.** That is enough to interrupt the loop — create a moment of space — and in that space a new pattern can begin.

> Every time you choose a better thought you are rewiring. Slowly. Deliberately. Permanently.

## Rule 20 — Tell It Better Things

Your Mind Has Been Listening to Everything You Have Ever Said About Yourself.

Every word. Every thought. Every quiet moment of self-doubt. It believed all of it.

### Your mind does not fact-check you.

It does not stop and say *"are you sure about that?"* It just accepts. Whatever you tell it, it takes as instruction. Then it works with everything it has to make that instruction real.

Every thought is either building you or dismantling you. **There is no neutral.**

### Every rule is either working for you or against you:

- Your mind does what it **thinks** you want.
- It responds to **pictures and words.**
- It loves what is **familiar.**
- It learns by **repetition.**

### The people who seem to have it together don't have a different mind.

They have been giving their mind **better instructions.** Better pictures. Better words. Better stories about who they are and what is possible.

### You can start today.

Not with a grand transformation. Not with a perfect mindset. Just one better thought than the one you had yesterday. Your mind learns by repetition. Repeat better things until they become the most familiar thing of all.

> Because your mind believes everything you tell it.
> **Tell it better things.**

## Rule 21 — You Make Your Beliefs and Your Beliefs Make You

The rule that ties all the others together.

### First you make the belief.

Nobody is born believing they are not enough. Those beliefs get *made* — out of moments, out of words, out of conclusions you mind has reached.

### Then the belief makes you.

Once a belief is set, it does not stay a thought in your head. It runs downhill through everything you do:

> **Thoughts → Feelings → Actions → Events**

Your thoughts control your feelings. Your feelings control your actions. Your actions control your events. So the belief you hold quietly at the top becomes the life you are actually living at the bottom.

| The belief at the top | The life it builds at the bottom |
|---|---|
| "I might be rejected" | You avoid the room, the risk, the ask |
| "I am not enough" | You chase more and never feel full |
| "I am enough" | You act like someone who already is |

You do not build the life you *want.* You build the life you *believe.* And what you expect almost always tends to be realized.

### Which means you get to change twice.

Here is the freedom hiding inside it. If you made the belief, you can make a different one — and you change on two levels, not one.

1. **Change the thinking** — go to the belief at the top, not the behaviour at the bottom.
2. **Change the behaviour** — the new belief runs downhill on its own: new feelings, new actions, new events.

If you want to control any behaviour at all, do not start with the behaviour. Start with the belief underneath it.

You are not the beliefs you were handed. You are the one who makes them — so make better ones, and let them make a better you.

> You made your beliefs. Now let your beliefs make you — on purpose this time.
