RTT® Framework Overview

What RTT Is

  • Rapid Transformational Therapy® by Marisa Peer — 30+ years of clinical development
  • 1–3 session model; combines hypnotherapy, NLP, psychotherapy, CBT
  • 3 parts: Find WHY (detective work) → Extract & Replace (dentist analogy) → Install (transformation recording)

The 4 I’s

  1. INVESTIGATE — Intake, Induction, Suggestibility, Deepening, Detective Work, Child in the Room
  2. INTERPRET — Left Hand/Right Hand, RFPI
  3. INTERRUPT — It’s Not Me, Dialogue, Child tools, Reframe
  4. INSTALL — Transformation recording, CCT, Healing Vortex, Cheerleader

Absolute Contraindications — NEVER Work With

  • Psychosis
  • Schizophrenia
  • Schizoaffective disorder
  • Personality disorders
  • Dissociative disorders
  • Epilepsy
  • Hallucinations (active)
  • Bipolar disorder (unless additionally qualified)
never

NEVER advise any client to stop or adjust medication.

What RTT Helps

  • Addictions & habits
  • Anxiety & stress
  • Eating disorders & weight
  • Depression & low mood
  • Confidence & self-esteem
  • Career & performance
  • Sexual & fertility issues
  • Pain management
  • Relationship patterns
  • Sleep problems
  • Phobias & fears

3 Competency Levels

  • Developing — Working toward the basics; still learning fundamentals
  • Attaining — Executing fundamentals reliably and consistently
  • Excelling — Artistry and intuition; seamlessly adaptive

3 Types of Change

  • Immediate — Instant shift; client feels different right away
  • Cumulative — Small improvements over time
  • Retroactive — Doesn’t realize until triggered or someone points it out

Key Numbers & Timelines

  • 21 days minimumrecording listening period

Setting Client Up for Success

What to Tell the Client Before Starting

  • “Don’t expect to feel hypnotized”
  • “Do expect to feel relaxed”
  • “You ARE in control at all times”
  • “There is no right or wrong way to experience this”
  • “You will be MORE aware, not less”
  • “You cannot get stuck in hypnosis”

Environment Setup

  • Remove personal/distracting items from background
  • Position client at 90-degree angle
  • Comfortable temperature and lighting
  • Pets out; family undisturbed for 2 hours
  • No deliveries; landline off; mobile silent
  • Strong Wi-Fi connection
  • Background music or white noise optional
  • Show bathroom location before starting

Items to Have Ready

  • Spare intake forms
  • Recording device with charger
  • Clipboard and pens
  • Highlighter (for client keywords)
  • Notepad
  • Tissues
  • Water and glasses
  • Blanket and cushions
note

Send a relaxation audio to client before the session. Send it after the discovery call.

careful

Intake / Consultation

Intake Sections

  1. Presenting Problem
  2. Symptoms / Triggers / Habits
  3. Childhood (background)
  4. What You Want — Magic Wand Question
  5. Life Without the Problem (future pacing)

Key Rules

  • Narrow down issue within the first 10 minutes
  • Only one presenting problem per session
  • Highlight keywords for use in Transformation recording
  • Listen for “ear prick-up moments” and trigger words

Magic Wand Questions

Use any of these — they’re interchangeable. Goal: get client to state their desired outcome in their own words.

“What do you want?”
“What do you want to get from this session?”
“If this was a magic wand and you could have one wish, what would it be?”
“When you leave here, what outcome do you want?”
“If I could grant you one wish, what would it be?”

Question Types to Use in Intake

  • Open — What, When, Where, How
  • Probing — digging deeper into responses
  • Clarifying — ensuring accurate understanding
  • Encouraging — affirming and opening up
  • Linking — connecting current to past

Chinese Whispers — When Client Doesn’t Know What They Want

If client can’t state what they want, use sentence completion:

“If I was free from the issue, I would…”
“I won’t allow myself to have a life without this issue because…”

If client says “I don’t know” during scenes:

“You do know. If you did know, what would you know?”

Induction

What to Look for (REM)

  • REM — Rapid Eye Movement under closed lids
  • Inward focus / stillness
  • Physical relaxation (jaw drop, slumped shoulders)
  • Slower breathing

Process

  1. Single rapid induction
  2. Watch for REM as confirmation of trance
  3. If client opens eyes: “That’s right, now close your eyes and go deeper”

Self-Hypnosis Steps (mod04)

  1. Look up toward eyebrows
  2. Breathe in 3 times deeply
  3. Close eyes on the 3rd exhale
  4. Drop chin to chest
  5. Imagine 10 steps going down
  6. Imagine a string on little finger — feel it lifting and tingling
  7. Give yourself suggestions
  8. Count 1–5 to bring back to full awareness
note

Looking up shifts brain from beta → alpha — the suggestible state.

Suggestibility Test 1: Bucket & Balloon

Arms out, palms up. Left hand holds heavy bucket of water (filling); right hand holds balloon (filling with helium). Watch arms drift — down left, up right.

“Your left arm is getting heavier and heavier as the bucket fills with water… your right arm is getting lighter and lighter as the balloon fills with helium…”

Suggestibility Test 2: Attracting Magnets

Hands in fists facing each other, about 6 inches apart. Suggest they’re powerful magnets drawing together.

“Your hands are like two powerful magnets being drawn together… the closer they get, the stronger the pull…”

If Suggestibility Tests Aren’t Working

“I can always tell when you’re responding, and you are.”

Never make the client feel they’ve failed. Reframe as success.

For controlling personalities (military, police, controlling parent background):

“I’m choosing to let this in. This is not control. This person is helping me.”

Ideomotor Finger Questioning (Self-Hypnosis)

  • Right hand finger lifts = YES
  • Left hand finger lifts = NO

Suggestion Rules

  • Always use present tense
  • Create vivid mental pictures
  • Avoid “no,” “not,” “don’t,” “try”
  • Focus on what you DO want, not what you don’t want

Deepening Techniques

Available Deepeners

  • Finger clicks — “go deeper” (with each click)
  • Hypnotic surrender — letting go completely
  • Arm drop — lift arm, drop suddenly
  • Eye lock — “eyelids are glued/sealed/locked tight”
  • Head rock — gentle side-to-side; maximum 10 seconds

Recovery If Client Opens Eyes

“That’s right, now close your eyes and go deeper.”

Brain Wave Goal

From beta (normal) → alpha (suggestible, relaxed). Eye roll up shifts brain state rapidly.

Detective Work / Daisy Chaining

Process

  1. Count backwards 5 to 1 to take client to scenes
  2. Get minimum 3 scenes linked to presenting issue
  3. Solidify each scene — establish details below
  4. Tap forehead 1–2 times to associate (snap fingers if online)
  5. Chain scenes back to presenting issue — this is Daisy Chaining

Solidify Each Scene — Ask:

  • Day or night?
  • Inside or outside?
  • Alone or with others?
  • How old are you?
  • What are you feeling?

Key Phrases During Detective Work

“You can feel this like it happened yesterday.”
“You do know. If you did know, what would you know?”
“Tell me, because I know you know.”

Associated vs. Dissociated

note

Dissociated = “I see myself at age 6” (viewing from outside). Must correct.

Associated = “I am 6 years old” (from inside). This is the goal.

“Be that 6-year-old now. Step into that scene.”

What NOT to Do in Detective Work

  • Never try more than twice if no scenes appear
  • Never say client is “not ready” or “resistant”
  • Never lead the client to specific content

If No Scenes After 2 Attempts → Switch To:

  • Child in the Room
  • RFPI (Interpret stage)

Abreaction Management

Abreaction = involuntary emotional reaction during regression. Let them shake or cry — never suppress it. Move client to a safe space.

“You are reviewing the scene, not reliving it.”
“You are in a safe space with me and the event is in the past.”
“You are in control.”
“Breathe deeply and remain calm.”

Child in the Room

When to Use

  • When no scenes appear after 2 attempts at Detective Work
  • As an alternative route to childhood material

Process

  1. Guide client to their childhood home, ages 2–10
  2. Take them to their childhood bedroom
  3. Have them meet their younger self
  4. Ask: “Are you happy?” — hesitation or pause = NO
  5. Explore: “What makes you unhappy?”
  6. Content is usually about parents/caregivers
note

The Child in the Room technique bridges naturally into the Interpret and Interrupt stages — particularly Upgrading the Child, Becoming the Loving Parent, and Merge the Child.

Left Hand / Right Hand — Process

Setup

Palms up, like holding two dinner plates.

  • Left hand = holds the 3 childhood scenes
  • Right hand = holds the current presenting issue

Client’s Job

  • Client works out the connection — therapist NEVER leads
  • This process is called Daisy Chaining
  • Client links scenes back to present issue organically

If Client Gets Stuck

  1. Ask for one word that links all three scenes (abandonment, despair, shame, etc.)
  2. Use Google search metaphor: “If you searched for these three scenes, what one word comes up?”
  3. Use ticker tape: “If these scenes ran as a ticker tape, what’s the headline?”
  4. Use essay metaphor: “If you wrote an essay about these scenes, what would the title be?”
careful

If client truly cannot make the connections → must go to RFPI.

Connecting to Core Beliefs

The link between scenes will always trace back to one of the 3 core beliefs:

“I’m not enough.”
“It’s not available to me.”
“I’m different. I don’t belong. I can’t connect.”

Childhood Needs Behind All Scenes

  • To feel significant

  • To feel like they matter
  • To feel celebrated
  • To make others proud

RFPI — Role, Function, Purpose, Intention

Process

  1. Have client speak AS the part — step into it
  2. Begin with: “I’m the part that came up with…”
  3. Ask all four questions — same underlying query, four different angles
  4. Always ask the age when the part came into existence (tap head)
  5. Thank the part before asking it to leave

The 4 RFPI Questions

  • “What is your Role?”
  • “What is your Function?”
  • “What is your Purpose?”
  • “What is your Intention?”
note

If client is confused by these words, replace with “Job”.

The Only 3 Reasons Parts Exist (3 P’s)

  1. Protect — keeping the person safe from perceived threat
  2. Punish — self-punishment or punishing others
  3. Prioritize Self / Get Attention — secondary gain

Key RFPI Phrases

“Tell me, because I know you know.”
“Thank you for trying to help me the way you did.”
“I don’t need you anymore — I’m an adult now.”
“You can go now.”

If Part Won’t Leave

  • Negotiate — give the part a new, more helpful role
  • Ask: “What new job would help me today?”
  • Watch for secondary gain

For Children

“If [the issue] had a job and wanted to be your friend, what would its job be?”

It’s Not Me

Rules

  • Must say minimum 9 times total (3 times minimum per scene)
  • Use the client’s own exact words
  • State with full conviction and emphasis
  • Emphasize they are no longer a dependent child

Script Template

“It’s not me. It really isn’t. It’s not me that [client’s words about the belief/event]. That was then and this is now. I am no longer a helpless, dependent child. It’s not me.”

Key Language Points

  • “You’re no longer a helpless, dependent child”
  • “You have a voice now”
  • “You came through your parents, not from them”
  • “The universe created you”
  • “Everything is available to you now”

Feelings Philosophy

note

Feelings are either in or out. They must be expressed to be released.

note

Anger is a protective mechanism with hurt underneath. Always find the hurt behind the anger.

Key Phrases

“You came through your parents, not from them.”
“The universe created you.”
“It’s never too late to have a happy childhood.”
“You can never go back, not even in your daydreams.”

Dialogue With the Hurter

When to Use

  • Only when someone caused emotional pain through words or actions
  • Express hurt behind the anger first
  • Switch back and forth: client ↔ hurter

Triple A Method

  1. Aware — bring awareness to what happened
  2. Accept — accept that it happened
  3. Articulate — express it fully

Order

careful

Forgiveness comes LAST, not first. Never assume forgiveness is required.

CRITICAL RESTRICTION

never

For sexual or physical abuse:
Client speaks TO the abuser.
Client does NOT speak WITH them.

Do not do the dialogue (switching back and forth) for sexual or physical abusers.

Upgrading the Child

careful

Must always include Merge the Child at the end.

  1. Show the child their adult life as it is now
  2. Emphasize: “Everything is available to you now”
  3. Install the child into the current world, not the past

Becoming the Loving Parent

Script

“No one can play this role like I can. I love you exactly the way you are. I love you completely and unconditionally.”

Purpose

Teach the client to meet their own needs — to parent themselves with love and without conditions.

Praise You’ve Always Wanted

  1. Ask client what they always wanted to hear as a child
  2. Have them say it to themselves (self-praise)
  3. Write it down for the Transformation recording
good sign

Self-praise is more powerful than external praise. The client giving it to themselves creates deeper neural impact.

Merge the Child

Process

  1. Child hovers one foot above the adult
  2. Child slowly descends into adult body
  3. Can merge one ageless child OR all children from all scenes

Key Phrase (Mandatory)

“They can never, ever go back to the way things used to be. Not even in your daydreams.”
good sign

Child takes up permanent residence in adult self.

The Reframe

Rules

  • Always comes JUST BEFORE the Transformation
  • NEVER include Reframe in the recording
  • Rock client’s head gently (or have them sway online)
  • Use a strong, commanding tone
  • Everything negative is “shrinking, fading, disappearing”

Reframe Language Pattern

“Constant, continuous, everlasting [positive state]…”

“Cannot, will not, does not influence you ever again…”

“Erasing, eradicating, eliminating, removing [the old belief/pattern]…”

Transition After Reframe

Move directly into the Transformation opening without a break. The Reframe is the runway for the Install stage.

Sequence Check

  1. Merge the Child ✓
  2. The Reframe ← you are here
  3. Transformation (Install) ↓

Transformation — Mandatory Opening Script

“And as you go deeper, understanding is power, and understanding in hypnosis is the most liberating, the most freeing, the most transforming power…”

What Follows the Opening

  1. Get client to look around, through, and behind the scenes
  2. Show them they created these beliefs — so they can transform them
  3. Give positive suggestions using intake keywords and session content
  4. Tell them they love listening to the recording daily
  5. Bring to full awareness

Transformation — Mandatory Ending Script

“You love to play this recording. You play it all the time because it has such a powerful, transforming, wonderful effect on you. You make the time and take the time to listen because its effects are so noticeable. The more you play your recording the better you feel, the better you feel, the more you play it. As my voice goes with you it has the most powerful, permanent and transforming impact on you.”
careful

This ending is mandatory — do not omit it from any transformation recording.

Transformation Language Rules

What TO Do

  • Use client’s exact words and metaphors from intake
  • Flip negatives to positive (see example below)
  • Present tense: “you are” not “you will be”
  • Powerful, vivid, descriptive language
  • Repetitive words and phrases for anchoring
  • For future goals: “you’re becoming…”
note

Example flip: “I don’t want to feel anxious” → “You remain completely calm, totally relaxed, and absolutely at ease.”

What NOT to Include in Recording

  • Painful or negative content from sessions
  • Negatives (don’t, not, never, no)
  • Past tense language
  • What they’re leaving behind
  • The Reframe (done live only)

Technical Details

  • 15–20 minutes maximum length
  • Phone on airplane mode
  • Exception: Insomnia — do NOT count out; tell them to stay sleeping
  • All other sessions: count 1–5 to bring to full awareness

Count Out Script (1–5)

“In a moment I’m going to count from 1 to 5. At the count of 5, open your eyes, feeling awake, alert, refreshed, energized, and feeling wonderful. 1… 2… 3… 4… 5. Eyes open, wide awake, feeling fantastic.”
careful

For insomnia sessions — do not count out. Instead tell client to stay sleeping and drift into natural sleep.

Installing the Cheerleader

Purpose

  • Raise self-esteem
  • Replace the inner critic
  • Install a permanent positive inner voice

Process

  1. Ask: “If you had a cheerleader, what would they say?”
  2. Keep asking: “What else?” until client says “That’s it.”
  3. Install the voice with pom-poms repeating the positive phrases

Template Options for Cheerleader

  • Best friend
  • Loving grandmother
  • Amazing teacher / mentor

Result

Client internalizes this voice and can access it whenever the inner critic appears. The cheerleader is a permanent installation.
